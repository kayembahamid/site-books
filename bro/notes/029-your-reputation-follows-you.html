Your Reputation Follows You

Note 029 of 62 · 262 words · Find Your Way Into The Real World

Every job leaves something behind.

Not just money. Not just another line on your CV. An impression. What your name means to someone. Whether you made things easier. Whether you'd be worth working with again.

Two people can do the same job and leave completely different impressions. Both finish the task. Both hit the deadline. But one makes the work easier and the other does their part and disappears.

Neither is terrible. Over time the difference gets impossible to ignore.

I thought it came down to skill. Usually it doesn't.

Potential means very little if no one has seen it. I've worked with smart people, talented people, people with great ideas. When chances appeared, they didn't always get them.

I thought promotions went to the most talented person. Then I watched the most talented person get passed over.

People promote who they trust. Can they rely on you? Can they hand you responsibility? Can they count on you when it gets difficult?

That's what they're actually watching.

The manager you have today may be a reference tomorrow. The coworker beside you may be somewhere else next year. The person you help today may open a door in five.

When you're young, jobs feel separate. Life doesn't work that way. Names come back.

Every job gives you two things. Skills and a reputation. The skills help you do the next job. The reputation helps you get it.

Years later someone may forget exactly what you worked on. They'll remember whether they trusted you.

Trust keeps making chances long after the job is over.
