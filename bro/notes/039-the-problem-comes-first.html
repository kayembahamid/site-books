The Problem Comes First

Note 039 of 62 · 215 words · Learn What Money Is Really Doing

"This takes too long."

That sounds like a complaint. So does "why is this so complicated," and "I wish there was an easier way."

Sometimes a complaint is more than a complaint. Sometimes it's a problem waiting for someone to notice it.

Spend time around business owners, freelancers, teachers, people building things, and you start hearing a different kind of conversation. They talk about problems. Not money. Problems.

A customer struggling with something. A process taking too long. A task that keeps causing frustration.

I didn't think much of it at first. Then the pattern got hard to ignore. The people creating the most value were paying attention to problems everyone else walked past.

Once you see that, complaints sound different. To one person it's ordinary noise. To another it's a clue.

That difference matters, because most successful businesses don't start with inspiration. They start with frustration. A problem that needs solving. A process that needs improving.

Most chances start with listening closely. What annoys people? What slows them down? What wastes their time?

The problem comes first. The solution comes later.

And once you look at the world that way you start seeing chances in places you'd never noticed. Not because they appeared. Because you finally started paying attention to what was already broken.
