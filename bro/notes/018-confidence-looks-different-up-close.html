Prepare before you perform.

Confidence Looks Different Up Close

Note 018 of 62 · 228 words · Start With Yourself

Confidence looks simple from a distance.

Someone walks into a room. Speaks clearly. Answers the questions. Handles the pressure. And you think: they're just confident.

You're seeing the last five minutes. Not the preparation that made them possible.

A student once told me: "Ham, you're always confident when you teach."

I laughed, because confidence had very little to do with it.

What he didn't see were the hours before class. Planning. Swapping activities around. Working out how to explain the hard part. Guessing what they'd ask.

By the time I walk into that classroom I'm not running on confidence. I'm running on preparation.

Same thing everywhere. The student who stays calm in an exam. The person who interviews well. The presenter who looks comfortable on stage.

From far away it looks like confidence. Up close it's almost always preparation.

Most of us do it backwards. We wait for the pressure to arrive. The night before the exam. The week before the interview. The day before the deadline.

Then we panic, because we're trying to prepare and perform at the same time. That rarely goes well.

Confidence is usually preparation in disguise. The work got done long before anybody noticed it.

So learn things before you need them. Opportunities have a habit of turning up without warning.

And it's much easier to answer the door when you're already dressed.
